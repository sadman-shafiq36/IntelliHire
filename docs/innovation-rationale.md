# Innovation feature rationale: the candidate feedback composer

## The problem

IntelliHire, like most hiring tools, is built for the company. It screens, scores, interviews and
assesses, and every conclusion is traceable to evidence. The candidate never sees any of that
evidence. Most rejected candidates hear nothing at all; the rest get a template that says nothing.
That isn't because recruiters don't care. Writing specific feedback for every candidate takes
time, and a careless sentence ("we wanted someone more energetic", "you ranked fifth") can expose
the company to a discrimination claim. So feedback is the step teams skip, and candidates come away
feeling the process was a black box.

## Who benefits, and how

- **Candidates** get a short, specific and kind explanation: what they showed well and what
  didn't come through, tied to the job's real requirements. A candidate who heard *why* can
  improve, and is more likely to apply again.
- **Recruiters and hiring managers** get a checked draft in seconds instead of a blank page, and
  can review a whole batch of screening letters in one queue.
- **The company** gets safer communication. Fixed rules and an AI judge block protected
  characteristics and their proxies, scores, rankings, comparisons with other candidates, promises
  and offer or salary content before a person ever sees the draft. Nothing is sent without
  approval, and every action is audited.

## Why it adds value here and not elsewhere

The composer writes nothing new about the candidate. It reuses evidence the platform already
verified: screening results with quotes checked against the CV, Q&A pairs mapped to requirements,
and the consolidated assessment's findings. Code chooses every point, and each one names a real
requirement and the stored row behind it, so the letter is as traceable as the assessment. The
same evidence that justifies the company's decision now explains it, honestly and safely, to the
person it affects.
## Why it was prioritised over the alternatives


- **A candidate self-service portal** (status tracking, rescheduling) would need candidate accounts
  and identity flows for little evidence-based value, and scheduling is already automated.
- **A bias-audit dashboard** across many hires needs far more data than one demo can produce, so
  its value couldn't be shown.
- **Automated reference checks** would mean contacting third parties about real people, which
  conflicts with the BID's privacy constraints.

The composer was the option with a real, common gap, a clear beneficiary outside the hiring team,
direct reuse of the platform's verified evidence, and a demonstrable result within the timeline.
It also shows the project's central idea at its sharpest: the AI drafts and evidences, people
decide.
